\chapter{Abstract}
\label{ch:abstract}

Systems with inter-related components are often modeled as networks, be it in the natural sciences biology and physics or the social sciences. Of particular interest are transport processes in these controlled systems or networks--- often studies by flow optimization techniques. This work focuses on the transport in networks with reactions, alternatively termed \emph{pathways} in \emph{reaction networks}. 

The size, intricate connectivity and combinatorial complexity of reaction networks makes transport processed in them difficult to be analysed manually. Specifically, possible pathways to a specified target molecule given a set of available source molecules, which simultaneously satisfy the laws of nature, is a common query to a reaction network.
This work attempts to integrate well-established computational tools from chemistry and physically informed modeling with the existing algorithmic methods to query pathways in reaction networks. It extends the available executable graph transformation language further to bring the exploration of reaction networks closer to reality.

The search for pathways that optimize the formation of a particular target molecule in a reaction network is a key problem in many settings, including reactor systems. Chemical reaction networks are mathematically well represented as hypergraphs, modeling that facilitates the search for pathways by computational means. This work presents a mixed-integer linear programming (mixed ILP) formulation of the search problem for pathways with the chemical potentials and concentrations for individual molecules integrated, enabling the user to constrain the search to return pathways containing only thermodynamically favorable reactions. Moreover, if multiple possible pathways are found, they can be ranked by an objective functions based on thermodynamics. 

The computational methodology for searching for synthetic pathways to target molecules in reaction networks based on integer linear programming is further extended by annotating the networks with information on the kinetics of the individual reactions. Often multiple pathways fit the given search criteria. To rank them, an objective function is developed based on physical arguments maximizing the probability of the pathway. This results in an automated pipeline to facilitate a flexible and kinetically informed investigation of pathways in large reaction networks by computational means, even for networks coming without kinetic annotation, such as those created via generative approaches for expanding molecular spaces. 

Lastly, the bare outline of modeling of natural phenomenon and describing the process is not sufficient. The sophisticated model must be accompanied by relevant example studies, demonstrating its relative strengths and shortcomings. Each method presented in this work is accompanied by an application, showcasing its practical applicability. 
The framework with integrated thermodynamics was applied to a reaction network representing the HCN-formamide chemistry. Alternative pathways to the one currently hypothesized in the literature are queried and enumerated, including some that score better according to the chosen objective function. 
A chemical reaction network generated from 2-hydroxyethanenitrile (glycolonitrile), water, and ammonia was annotated with energy barriers and pathways to glycine and 2-hydroxyethanoic acid (glycolic acid) were searched for using the formulated method.
